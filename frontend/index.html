Orbit-X

Flip through NASA SPHEREx infrared images to find things that move or change.

How to spot a moving or changing object

  1. Pick a spot. Type a name (Barnard's Star, R Dra) or coordinates (133.795 -7.245 or 08h55m10.8s -07d14m42s), choose a field of view, press Load. Names are moved by their proper motion to the SPHEREx date, so fast stars are centred.
  2. Watch the flipbook. Each frame stacks all images from one ~6-month SPHEREx visit. A star that jumps between frames is moving (nearby stars, brown dwarfs); one that changes brightness is variable.
  3. Use Difference. Each visit minus the first: still objects vanish to grey, a mover becomes a black/white pair (black = where it was, white = where it is). A − B subtracts any two visits. Deep adds all visits together to see fainter things.
  4. Pick a colour band. SPHEREx splits 0.75–5 µm into 102 channels over 6 detectors. D1 (0.75–1.1 µm) … D6 (4.4–5.0 µm) stack each detector; RGB maps short → blue, long → red, so very cold objects like brown dwarfs look red.
  5. Click any star in the frame to get its spectrum (brightness vs wavelength) and light curve (brightness vs time), computed on the fly from the images.
  6. Compare with Gaia (green circles; orange = fast movers with a 10-year arrow) and WISE (purple), the sky map below, or WiseView (10 years of WISE).

Keys: Space play/pause · ←/→ step · D difference · B blink. Share or embed: every search updates the page URL (?name=… or ?ra=…&dec=…&fov=…); add &embed=1 for a compact view to put in an <iframe>.
